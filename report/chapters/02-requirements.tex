can link each one to its implementation, its tests and its place in the demo. The
\emph{Source} column cites the requirements document (R) or the blueprint (B, with section).

NFR-07 & Maintainability & Layered architecture enforced by import contracts; \texttt{mypy
  --strict}; at least 80\,\% test coverage on the domain and services. \\
NFR-08 & Portability & Starts with one command (Docker Compose), and also natively on Windows,
  Linux and macOS with uv, npm and SQLite; the same code runs on SQLite and PostgreSQL. \\

\section{Business rules}

\begin{longtable}{@{}l>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{12cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{ID} & \textbf{Rule} \\
\midrule
\endhead
BR-01 & Employees see and act only on their own incidents. \\
BR-02 & Only the assignee, the escalation owner or an administrator may acknowledge, update,
  request information or resolve. \\
BR-03 & Only the reporter verifies or rejects a fix; unanswered resolutions auto-close after
  72 hours. \\
BR-04 & Resolving requires a resolution summary. Reopening is allowed only from RESOLVED; a
  problem after closure becomes a new, linked incident. \\
BR-05 & Priority is computed; overrides need a reason and are audited, and category floors
  apply. \\
BR-06 & A technician's weighted load stays at or below 10 points (P1--P4 cost 4, 3, 2, 1), and
  unavailable technicians are never auto-assigned. \\
BR-07 & The SLA clock pauses in ON\_HOLD, the response SLA is met at first acknowledgement, and
  a breach means now $>$ deadline. \\
BR-08 & Unacknowledged P1 and P2 incidents escalate one level per elapsed response target. \\
BR-09 & A suggested duplicate is offered at similarity $\geq \theta$; a near-identical ticket
  ($\geq \theta_{\text{high}}$) from the same reporter within 30 minutes is refused with a link
  to the existing one. \\
BR-10 & Audit records are append-only, and incidents are never hard-deleted. \\
\bottomrule
\end{longtable}

\subsection*{Rule parameters}

Priority score $S = I \cdot U \cdot C$ with $I, U, C \in \{1, 2, 3\}$:

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Priority} & \textbf{Score} & \textbf{Possible scores} & \textbf{Combinations of 27} \\
\midrule
P1 Critical & $S \geq 18$ & 18, 27 & 4 \\
P2 High & $9 \leq S < 18$ & 9, 12 & 6 \\
P3 Medium & $4 \leq S < 9$ & 4, 6, 8 & 10 \\
P4 Low & $S < 4$ & 1, 2, 3 & 7 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

SLA targets (response / resolution). Gold is the requirements' table; Silver and Bronze are
$\times 2$ and $\times 4$. A service's default tier follows its criticality (3 Gold, 2 Silver,
1 Bronze).

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Priority} & \textbf{Gold} & \textbf{Silver} & \textbf{Bronze} \\
\midrule
P1 & 5 min / 30 min & 10 min / 1 h & 20 min / 2 h \\
P2 & 15 min / 2 h & 30 min / 4 h & 1 h / 8 h \\
P3 & 1 h / 8 h & 2 h / 16 h & 4 h / 32 h \\
P4 & 4 h / 24 h & 8 h / 48 h & 16 h / 96 h \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

An incident's SLA status is AT\_RISK once 75\,\% of its target has elapsed (inclusive); this
status drives search, the dashboard and the monitor's warning. Separately, staff see a predictive
breach risk, flagged when the estimate from comparable past incidents reaches 0.5, with the
75\,\% rule as its fallback when fewer than ten are comparable (D-032).
