The requirements PDF (docs/source/OOP_S1_2026-2027_Project.pdf) wins every conflict.

| D-029 | 11 Nov | Nobody creates, changes or deactivates an account whose role is above their own escalation level, so only an IT Manager manages IT Managers. | D-030 | 12 Nov | Duplicate detection compares a report with incidents still open (NEW to ON_HOLD) reported in the last 14 days, at most the 300 newest. Gemini's vectors are stored per incident in `incident_embeddings`, tagged with provider and model; TF-IDF vectors are recomputed per check over those recent incidents and never stored. θ = 0.49 and θ-high = 0.89 come from 52 hand-labelled pairs (`scripts/tune_duplicates.py`, `docs/testing/duplicates.md`); a test fails if the constants drift from the tuning. | TF-IDF weights depend on the corpus, so a stored TF-IDF vector cannot be compared with a later one; §6.2 says "stored per incident", which holds for the embeddings that are comparable. Bounding the window keeps the check inside the 300 ms target. | §6.2, §9 |
| D-031 | 12 Nov | "Follow this instead" records the report and cancels it at once as a duplicate of the chosen incident, with the reporter as actor; only an incident the similarity check itself suggested may be followed. Any staff member who may work an incident (BR-02) may confirm it as a duplicate. A parent must exist, be open, and not itself be a duplicate, and an incident that already has duplicates cannot become one. The reporter of a linked duplicate sees the parent's id, title and status and is notified when it is resolved; they still cannot open the parent. Amended after the P4 review: a reporter is shown, and may follow, at most the three best matches; a parent must be still open (not resolved or closed); an incident others follow cannot be withdrawn or cancelled, only resolved; staff may link only to a parent they may view or one shown to them as a possible duplicate of that incident. | §6.2 does not say what following does or who confirms. Following is how an employee comes to hear about someone else's incident, so it is limited to what was already shown to them (BR-01); one level of linking keeps "subscribed to the parent" (D-005) meaningful. | §6.2, BR-01, BR-02, BR-09 |
| D-032 | 12 Nov | The §6.3 estimate is served per incident (`GET /incidents/{id}/risk`) and shown to staff as a chip with its reason; the reporter sees only the SLA chip. The stored SLA status (search filter, dashboard "at risk" counts and the monitor's 75 % warning) keeps the §5.2 threshold. Comparable history is the newest 1000 resolved incidents (RESOLVED, VERIFIED, CLOSED) in the same category and priority, working time excluding pauses; a running clock is measured against its live target. Every incident response now carries its duplicate links, not only the detail read. **Needs the team's confirmation.** | The status filters must stay indexable SQL (D-018) and the monitor's warning once per cycle; estimating every open incident on each 30 s tick would also make warnings depend on history that changes underneath. Moving the monitor's warning onto the estimate is a contained change if the team wants it. | §5.2, §6.3, §9 |
| D-033 | 12 Nov | Live updates (P4.8): one socket at `/ws` (`/api/ws` through the proxies). The first message carries the access token; anything else, or nothing within 10 s, closes it with 4401. Pushes say only `{"type": "incident", "incident_id", "status"}` and go, after the commit, to the reporter, the assignee and anyone who may view all or a team's incidents; the browser refetches through the API. Polling stays as a safety net: 60 s while the socket is up, 10 s while it reconnects (1 s backoff doubling to 30 s), with a banner saying so. Replaces D-024's 10 s polling. Amended after the P4 review: an open socket re-checks its token every 30 s and closes with 4401 once it has expired, the user is deactivated or their view changes; followers of a duplicate are pushed their own incident's id when the parent changes. | A token in the URL ends up in proxy logs. The hub is in-process, which is right for one API instance (Render free); several instances would need a shared broker. | §6.4, §8, BR-01 |
| D-034 | 12 Nov | Telegram alerts (P4.9): a user links a chat by entering its numeric ID under "Telegram alerts" in the user menu (they start a chat with our bot and read their ID from @userinfobot); the bot does not poll for messages or hand out linking codes. Errors from the Bot API never include the request URL, because it carries the token. | Reading updates (a webhook or `getUpdates`) is the /report chatbot's job, which D-027 cut; entering the ID needs no running bot process on a free host that sleeps. | §5.4, §8 |
| D-035 | 12 Nov | Google Sign-In (P4.10): the browser gets an ID token from Google Identity Services and posts it to `POST /auth/google`. The API checks the RS256 signature against Google's published keys (PyJWT), the audience, the issuer, the expiry against the injected Clock and `email_verified`, then requires `hd` to equal `GOOGLE_HD_DOMAIN`. A first sign-in creates an Employee with no password; an existing account with the same email is linked to the Google account (`sub`), and a different Google account is refused for it afterwards. Offered only when `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID`, `GOOGLE_HD_DOMAIN` and `VITE_GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` are set. Amended after the P4 review: linking an account that has a password drops the password (audited), because registering one never proved the email was the registrant's; Google tokens' expiry is judged on the system clock, so the ×10 demo clock cannot reject them. | D-008 asks for `hd` to be checked always, so a deployment without a campus domain has no Google Sign-In rather than an open one. Linking by verified campus email lets staff created by an admin sign in with Google without a separate invitation. | §8, D-008 |
| D-036 | 12 Nov | Demo reset (P5.5): `python -m app.demo` (behind `make demo-reset`) wipes the database, migrates, seeds, runs one SLA-monitor tick with no notifier so the generated history's breaches, escalations and auto-closes are recorded before anyone watches, then stages Workflow 1 (an open P4 Fee Payment Portal report from another employee, so the live report shows the duplicate hint) and Workflow 2 (a P4 Wi-Fi incident from the demo employee in the demo technician's queue) through the IncidentService facade. Workflow 3 is not staged: a P1 staged before the take would escalate within 90 real seconds on the ×10 clock. `make demo-reset` then serves both apps with `APP_ENV=demo`, `CLOCK_MODE=accelerated` and no reload. It refuses `APP_ENV=prod`, and, because `make demo-reset` sets `APP_ENV=demo` itself, it also refuses any database that is not SQLite or PostgreSQL on localhost or Compose's `postgres` unless given `--allow-remote-database`, which only the image's `DEMO_RESET_ON_START` passes (P5 review). `app.demo` is a second composition root beside `app.main`; no layer may import it. The image's `SEED_ON_START` now uses `python -m app.demo --seed-if-empty`, which adds the same quiet catch-up to a first seed. A deployment stays on production settings (P5.1); `DEMO_RESET_ON_START=true` with `APP_ENV=demo` turns it into a demo instance that resets on every start (docs/DEPLOY.md). |
